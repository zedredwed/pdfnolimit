## The one thing that makes PDFNoLimit different

Most free PDF tools upload your document to a server, process it there, and send it back. Your contracts, payslips, medical records and ID scans travel across the internet and come to rest on somebody else's disk.

**PDFNoLimit never uploads your file.** Every operation — editing text, merging, splitting, compressing, converting, signing, redacting — runs in your own browser, on your own machine. The document is opened by the browser itself, not by a server.

### Don't take our word for it

That claim is easy to make and easy to fake, and plenty of tools make it while doing the opposite. So here is how to check it yourself, in under a minute. No technical knowledge required for the first method.

<table>
<tr><td width="50%" valign="top">

**Method 1 — the airplane test**

1. Open [www.pdfnolimit.com](https://www.pdfnolimit.com) and let the page finish loading.
2. Turn off your Wi-Fi, or switch on airplane mode.
3. Now open a PDF and edit it.

**It keeps working.** A tool that ships your file to a server physically cannot do this.

</td><td width="50%" valign="top">

**Method 2 — watch the network**

1. Press <kbd>F12</kbd> to open your browser's developer tools.
2. Go to the **Network** tab and filter on `Fetch/XHR`.
3. Load a PDF and run any tool.

**No request carries your document.** Nothing leaves the tab.

</td></tr>
</table>

This is the reason PDFNoLimit exists. Everything else on this page is a consequence of it.

## Reporting a bug

Bug reports are welcome and read. Open one in the [**Issues**](https://github.com/zedredwed/free-unlimited-pdf-tools/issues) tab.

To get a fix quickly, please include:

- **Which tool** you were using, and the page URL
- **What you expected**, and what happened instead
- **Your browser and version**, and your operating system
- **Whether it happens every time** or only with one specific file
- **The browser console output** if you can get it — <kbd>F12</kbd> → Console tab

**Please never attach a confidential PDF.** If the problem is tied to one file, describe it instead — how many pages, whether it is scanned, which language, whether it is password-protected, roughly how large. That is usually enough to reproduce it.

## Requesting a feature

Also the [Issues](https://github.com/zedredwed/free-unlimited-pdf-tools/issues) tab. Tell us the problem you are trying to solve rather than the button you want — it usually leads somewhere better.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary><b>Is it really free? What's the catch?</b></summary>

<br>

Yes — **100% free, with zero paywalls**. Every tool, for an unlimited number of files. No account, no trial that expires, no subscription, no "pro" tier, no page limit, and no watermark on your export.

There is no hidden step where a feature turns out to cost money.

**How it is paid for:** by the advertising displayed on the page. Not by a subscription, and not by your data — which is possible precisely because nothing is uploaded. No servers receiving millions of files means no server bill, and therefore no reason for a paywall.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>What happens to my files?</b></summary>

<br>

Nothing, because they never leave your computer. There is no upload, so there is no copy on a server to delete, leak or hand over. Close the tab and it is gone. See [the verification steps above](#dont-take-our-word-for-it) to confirm this yourself rather than believing it.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Why isn't the source code public?</b></summary>

<br>

PDFNoLimit is a commercial product, and the code stays private.

We are aware this sits oddly next to a privacy claim, so the claim is not built on trust: it is built on something you can observe from outside. Pull the network cable and the editor keeps working. Watch the Network tab and your document never appears in a request. Those two facts are verifiable without reading a single line of source, and they cannot be faked by a tool that uploads your file.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Does it work offline?</b></summary>

<br>

Once the page has loaded, yes. That is the clearest demonstration that processing is local.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Is there a file size or page limit?</b></summary>

<br>

There is **no page limit and no file size limit** imposed by us, and no daily quota counting down in a corner.

The only real ceiling is your own device's memory, since everything is held in the browser. Very large documents work; very large documents on an old phone may not.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>Can I use it for confidential or regulated documents?</b></summary>

<br>

That is precisely what it was built for — legal, medical, financial and HR documents that should not be sent to a third-party server. Verify the behaviour yourself first, and follow your own organisation's policy.

</details>
